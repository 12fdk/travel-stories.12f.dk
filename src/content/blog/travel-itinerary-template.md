# Free Travel Itinerary Template (CSV + Day-by-Day Guide)

A travel itinerary template is one row per day, with blocks for the morning, afternoon and evening, a Money column and a Notes column. The blank CSV below holds exactly that grid. The part that does the work is the empty evening on most rows.

A plan with no gaps loses to the first delay, because one late opening then rewrites the rest of the day by hand. You get the template with the worked five-day example to compare your own draft against, and a one-minute check that says whether your route is relaxed or over-loaded.

## What belongs in a travel itinerary template

Seven columns, in this order:

| Column | What goes in it | Why it's there |
|---|---|---|
| Day | 1, 2, 3, one per row | The row is the unit of the trip |
| Date | The calendar date, if the days are fixed | Lets the plan survive a flight delay without a rewrite |
| Morning | One main plan | Put the fixed-time plan here, since a booked slot is easier to keep in the morning |
| Afternoon | One main plan | Same rule: one plan per half-day |
| Evening | A plan, or left blank | The blank is a feature, explained below |
| Money | A daily spending pace to stay under | Keeps the day honest without turning it into a receipt log |
| Notes | Times, tickets, phone numbers, distances | The facts the morning-you won't remember from the sheet |

The template is a blank grid with exactly these columns. It has no formulas on purpose: an itinerary is a plan you change with your hands, and a grid that recalculates fights that. The Money column is the only one that carries a number. It tracks a daily pace rather than logging receipts, and the total belongs in the [travel budget template](/blog/travel-budget-template/), which tracks the whole trip by category.

## A worked example: five days in a city

The example is a five-day city trip on example dates, 2 to 6 March. It is the shape of a plan, not a report on any city.

| Day | Date | Morning | Afternoon | Evening | Money | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 Mar | Arrival: transfer to the place we are staying | A first walk of the neighborhood and the transit pass | Blank | $40 | Check-in from 3 pm; keep the luggage room ticket |
| 2 | 3 Mar | One museum, booked for 9:30 | The old town on foot, one square at a time | Blank | $60 | Museum closes at 2; the walk starts after lunch |
| 3 | 4 Mar | A market and the food that is best there | A viewpoint and the long walk down from it | A table we book in advance, 8 pm | $55 | The walk is long, so the afternoon holds one plan |
| 4 | 5 Mar | A full-day trip that leaves at 10 and is back by 6 | Continues | Blank | $70 | Book the return ride before we leave |
| 5 | 6 Mar | Pack, check out, and the transfer to the airport | The flight | Blank | $30 | The transfer needs 2 hours before departure |

Three things to read out of it:

- **Day 1 and day 5 carry the logistics.** The arrival day holds a transfer, a first walk and a pass. The departure day holds packing and the transfer, with the airport time in Notes. Two days of a five-day trip are logistics, and a plan that pretends otherwise loses a full day.
- **The middle days hold one main plan per half-day.** A museum in the morning, a walk in the afternoon. Day 4 is the one full exception: the day trip owns the whole day, and that is why it sits in the Morning cell with the Afternoon marked as continues.
- **Evenings stay blank except day 3.** One reserved table is the only evening plan in the trip, and it is the one item that had to be booked before the trip. That is the test for an evening: a reservation or a departure goes in, and everything you can decide that night stays out.

## Leave the evening open on purpose

A plan written end to end has no slack left for the first delay. One long queue or one late opening pushes back the next two days, the edits happen standing in line, and the rest of the trip runs on a version of the plan nobody is looking at.

The blank evening is the slack: it is the slot where the better table, the late opening and the second visit go without touching anything else. The rule for filling one is the day-3 test above: a reservation or a departure goes in, and everything you can decide that night stays out.

## Check your own itinerary against the rule

The rule is a count, and it runs in a minute. Go through your draft day by day: flag any day with more than one main plan in a half-day, or more than one evening plan. Flag day 1 or the last day if it holds more than a transfer and a short walk.

Apply it to the example above: the day-1 afternoon first walk is the one soft case, and it is the short walk the rule allows. A 10-day draft that passes on eight days still has two days to open up, and the fix is the same every time: move one main plan to a lighter day or into the evening slot.

## Where a travel itinerary template goes wrong

Four mistakes break an itinerary, whether the template is a sheet, a notebook or a group chat.

- **Three or four main plans on one day.** The first queue decides which plan survives, and the choice gets made at the worst possible time, standing in line. Cut the day to one plan per half-day before the trip, not after.
- **No travel time in the plan.** The two squares are 20 minutes apart, and the template says both in the same half-day. Write the walk or the ride in Notes with a realistic number, and let the plan carry it.
- **The budget lives in the itinerary.** A Money column that becomes a receipt log turns the plan into an audit. Keep the column a daily pace, and let the [budget template](/blog/travel-budget-template/) carry the category totals for the whole trip.
- **A plan that can't be changed on the ground.** If a dropped plan means rebuilding the sheet, the plan won't get updated and the day runs on the old version. Move it to the next open block, mark it cut in Notes, and stop.
